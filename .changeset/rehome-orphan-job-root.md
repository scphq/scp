A detached job told to set `SCP_UNDER=<agent>` now gets there when its name is already taken by a root of its own. An exact claim (`--as`) with `under` re-homes a root that only `--as` claims held, released, with no children on any machine and of the claim's harness or another variant of the same provider, to where a new endpoint of the claim would sit: under that agent, placed with its own rules kept or linked, audited `claim_rehome {target, parent}`. A link refused after the takeover refuses the whole claim. A row a session holds or held, one the claim's lineage places elsewhere and an agent the row may not link under keep the refusal. An exact claim refused because a live session holds the row now says so and names a way out: `me/<name> is held by a live session; a detached job can't act as it. Give the job its own name, e.g. SCP_UNDER=<name> swarm send --as <name>-wake ...`. The native broker's other exact refusals now read as the TypeScript broker's instead of `no available endpoint`. Both brokers behave alike.
